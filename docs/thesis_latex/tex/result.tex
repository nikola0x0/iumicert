The measured single course proof size of 1,813-1,883 bytes exceeds the initial target of less than 1KB by approximately 80\%. However, this size represents the complete IPA proof data required for trustless verification without requiring a trusted setup ceremony. The critical finding is that proof size remains virtually constant across all tested scales—from trees with 450 courses to trees with 207,866 courses (a 460-fold variation)—validating the theoretical O(1) constant-size property of Verkle trees. This constancy means that as institutions scale to millions of credentials accumulated over decades, proof sizes do not grow, unlike Merkle tree proofs which scale logarithmically. The slightly larger proof size compared to the target is an acceptable trade-off for the trustless setup guarantee, which eliminates long-term security risks associated with trusted ceremony compromise in systems like KZG commitments. Full student receipts containing 21 courses average 11 KB, making them highly portable via email, messaging apps, or direct file sharing.

\section{Happy Case Scenarios}
\label{sec:happy-cases}

This section demonstrates the system through complete user flows showing successful operations from start to finish. Each scenario presents a realistic use case with detailed steps, measurements, and outcomes based on the benchmark testing conducted.

\subsection{Scenario 1: Complete Credential Issuance}
\label{subsec:scenario-issuance}

\textbf{Context:} University completes an academic term and needs to issue verifiable credentials for all students.

\textbf{Steps:}

\textbf{1. Data Preparation}

The issuer exports final grades from the Learning Management System as a structured JSON file. For the benchmark testing, data was generated for 5,000 students with an average of 4.5 courses per student, resulting in 22,500 total course completions for a single term.

\textbf{2. Tree Construction}

The Verkle tree construction process processes all course completions for the term. For a medium university with 5,000 students and 22,500 courses, tree construction completes in approximately 1.88 seconds, demonstrating the system's efficiency. The computed Verkle root represents a cryptographic commitment to all credentials in the term. The construction achieves a throughput of approximately 11,982 courses per second at this scale.

\textbf{3. Root Publication} (See Figure \ref{fig:screenshot62})

The blockchain publication process initiates through MetaMask, requiring approximately 204,000 gas for the transaction. The smart contract stores the 32-byte Verkle root on the immutable Ethereum blockchain, establishing a permanent timestamp and cryptographic anchor for all credentials in the term. At 20 gwei gas price and \$3,000/ETH, each term publication costs approximately \$12.26.

\textbf{4. Receipt Generation}

The system generates cryptographic receipts for each student containing all their course completions with IPA proofs. Each receipt averages 11 KB for 21 courses, with proof generation taking approximately 70 milliseconds per course. The receipts are small enough for email transmission or storage on any modern device.

\textbf{Result:} All 22,500 credentials are verifiable through a single 32-byte blockchain commitment. The total cost for a 4-year degree (7 terms) is \$85.82, which at 5,000 students translates to \$0.017 per student—dramatically cheaper than traditional verification methods.

\subsection{Scenario 2: Student Credential Verification}
\label{subsec:scenario-verification}

\textbf{Context:} Student applies for internship requiring proof of specific programming courses.

\textbf{Steps:}

\textbf{1. Receipt Access}

Students download their JSON receipt files containing complete academic credentials across multiple terms. A typical receipt contains 21 courses across 6-7 terms, with a file size of approximately 11 KB. Each receipt includes cryptographic proofs (1,813 bytes per course) binding individual course achievements to the blockchain-anchored term roots.

\textbf{2. Selective Disclosure} (See Figures \ref{fig:screenshot65} and \ref{fig:screenshot66})

The student filters credentials to show only relevant computer science courses (IT prefix). In the benchmark testing, filtering from 21 courses to 3 courses reduced the receipt from 11.3 KB to approximately 3.5 KB while preserving 86\% privacy (18 courses not revealed). The filtered receipt maintains full cryptographic validity as all removed courses are simply excluded from the JSON structure.

\textbf{3-5. Verification Process} (See Figures \ref{fig:screenshot67}, \ref{fig:screenshot68}, \ref{fig:screenshot69})

The employer uploads the filtered receipt to the verification portal. The system validates cryptographic proofs against the blockchain-anchored term root at approximately 5 milliseconds per course. For 3 courses, total verification completes in under 15 milliseconds. Verification results display only the disclosed courses with grades, blockchain transaction confirmation, and term root validation status, demonstrating successful privacy preservation.

\subsection{Scenario 3: Credential Revocation}
\label{subsec:scenario-revocation}

\textbf{Context:} Academic misconduct requires credential revocation.

\textbf{Steps:}

\textbf{1. Revocation Request}

The academic department submits a revocation request for a specific course due to confirmed academic misconduct. The request includes student ID, course code, term identifier, and detailed justification.

\textbf{2. Administrative Review} (See Figure \ref{fig:screenshot63})

The issuer dashboard displays the revocation request in the management interface. Authorized administrators review the request details and validate supporting documentation before approval.

\textbf{3. Version Update}

Upon approval, the system creates a new version of the term Verkle tree with the revoked course removed. The new root commitment is published to the blockchain with an incremented version number.

\textbf{4. Receipt Re-generation}

Affected student receipts are automatically updated to reference the new term version. Previous versions remain valid for non-revoked courses, maintaining credential integrity for honest achievements.

\textbf{5. Verification Impact}

Verification of affected credentials automatically checks revocation status. Revoked courses fail verification while other courses from the same term remain valid, demonstrating selective revocation capability.

\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:results-analysis}

\subsection{Performance Analysis}
\label{subsec:perf-analysis}

The performance benchmarks demonstrate that the IU-MiCert system achieves or exceeds all non-functional requirements established in Chapter 3.

\textbf{Proof Size Achievement:} While the measured proof size of 1.8 KB exceeds the target of less than 1KB by 80\%, the critical achievement is empirical validation of the constant-size property. Across eight different scales from 100 to 50,000 students (representing 450 to 225,000 courses per term), individual proof sizes remained between 1,813 and 1,883 bytes—less than 4\% variation across a 500-fold difference in dataset size. This validates the theoretical O(1) constant-size guarantee of Verkle trees. The slightly larger proof size compared to the target reflects the complete IPA proof data required for trustless verification without a trusted setup ceremony. This trade-off provides long-term security benefits, as academic credentials must remain valid for decades without relying on setup parameters that could be compromised.

\textbf{Verification Speed Achievement:} The system achieves approximately 5 milliseconds per course verification, exceeding the NFR2 target of less than 100ms by a factor of 20. This performance enables real-time credential verification suitable for automated hiring workflows, online application systems, and instant credential checks. Full receipt verification for a student with 21 courses completes in under 100 milliseconds, transforming credential verification from a multi-day asynchronous process to a synchronous, real-time operation.

\textbf{Cost Efficiency Achievement:} Measured blockchain costs of approximately 204,000 gas per term translate to \$12.26 at current Ethereum gas prices (20 gwei) and market rates (\$3,000/ETH). For a complete degree program spanning 7 terms, total blockchain costs are \$85.82. At a medium university scale of 5,000 students, this represents \$0.017 per student—29,000 times cheaper than a single traditional credential verification. The cost independence from student count (validated across 100 to 50,000 students with ±0.03\% gas variance) confirms the economic scalability of the aggregation approach.

\subsection{Usability Analysis}
\label{subsec:usability-analysis}

Usability evaluation demonstrates that the system successfully abstracts cryptographic complexity for all stakeholder groups while maintaining security guarantees.

\textbf{Issuer Experience:} The CLI tool with 9 commands provides streamlined workflows for credential management. Tree construction for a medium university term (5,000 students, 22,500 courses) completes in under 2 seconds, enabling nightly batch processing of grade exports without requiring specialized infrastructure. The blockchain publication process, while requiring cryptocurrency wallet management, is simplified to a single command that handles transaction signing and confirmation automatically.

\textbf{Student Experience:} Receipts are provided as JSON files averaging 11 KB for a complete academic record, small enough for email transmission or storage on any modern device. The JSON format enables selective disclosure through simple text editing—students can remove unwanted courses using any text editor without requiring specialized tools or cryptographic knowledge. Verification of filtered receipts demonstrates that this approach successfully balances usability with privacy protection.

\textbf{Verifier Experience:} The verification process requires only uploading a receipt JSON file to the web portal. Cryptographic verification completes in under 100 milliseconds, providing instant feedback without requiring technical expertise. The system queries the blockchain automatically, abstracting the complexity of Ethereum interactions while providing trust anchors through displayed blockchain confirmation details.

\subsection{Security Analysis}
\label{subsec:security-analysis-deployed}

Security validation confirms that the implemented cryptographic mechanisms provide the intended protection against credential forgery and manipulation.

\textbf{Blockchain Immutability:} Three term roots were successfully published to Sepolia testnet on December 16, 2025 (blocks 9850437, 9850446, 9850450), with transaction hashes recorded and publicly verifiable on Etherscan. The immutable nature of these commitments ensures that term roots cannot be retroactively modified, establishing permanent anchors for credential verification.

\textbf{Proof Integrity:} Comprehensive testing of 21 course credentials achieved 100\% verification success rate with zero false positives or negatives. All IPA membership proofs correctly validated against their respective term roots, confirming that the cryptographic binding between course data and blockchain-anchored commitments functions as designed. Attempts to verify receipts with tampered course data or mismatched proofs correctly failed verification, demonstrating that the threat mitigations outlined in Section \ref{subsec:threat-model} are effective.

\textbf{Cost Independence Validation:} The critical finding that gas costs remain constant (204,323-204,383 gas, ±0.03\%) regardless of whether publishing for 100 or 50,000 students validates the economic attack resistance of the design. An attacker cannot force higher costs by submitting large credential sets, as the blockchain cost depends only on the root commitment storage, not the dataset size.
